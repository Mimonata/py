The book (English title: "You Look Like a Thing and I Love You") is fun and accessible but it's a pop-science book — it explains AI behavior and failures through humor, not how to actually build or understand the systems technically. It won't teach you neural networks or machine learning in any meaningful depth.

Is it still relevant? Partially. The core ideas about AI limitations hold up, but the AI landscape changed massively after 2019 — the book predates large language models like ChatGPT entirely.

For your actual goal (understanding AI to work in IT), I'd skip it and go with:

"Hands-On Machine Learning with Scikit-Learn, Keras & TensorFlow" by Aurélien Géron — the standard practical book, Python-based, teaches neural networks from scratch with code. Heavy but thorough.
fast.ai (free online course) — practical, Python, gets you building real models fast. Better for your learning style than a textbook.
For just understanding conceptually what neural networks are (no code), the 3Blue1Brown neural network series on YouTube (free, ~1 hour total) is the best explanation that exists — very visual, builds the intuition perfectly.

Given you're already learning Python at 42, fast.ai or 3Blue1Brown + Géron's book would be the most direct path to your goal.
